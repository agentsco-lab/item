# Step 21: the A/B against phosh, and wp_presentation

The question: is item-compositor to be item's base, in place of item on
phosh and phoc (item-shell 0.1.0 over the port)? Trying it by hand says
yes (steps 1-20); these are the numbers, taken the same way
under both stacks on the same phone the same night, 2026-10-01.

## How it was measured

Nothing of either compositor could be trusted to time the other, so the
clocks are below both, or in a client both serve alike:

- **`hwc2_compat_display_present`**, libhybris' call that hands hwcomposer
  a frame, which phoc and item-compositor both make: a kernel uprobe on it
  (`tools/ab-latency.sh`, `tools/ab-motion.sh`), in the tracer's `mono`
  clock, the tracer left as it was found. A present is the frame leaving
  the compositor; it shows at the vsync after, the same for both.
- **A probe client** (`probes/latency`, `latency-probe`): a window with a
  square that changes colour on each touch down (`tap`) or each frame
  (`anim`), damaging only the square, noting each commit. It also asks for
  `wp_presentation` feedback.
- **The virtual finger** (`tools/touch-script.py`) notes each touch down
  and each drag in CLOCK_MONOTONIC.
- **The shell at rest** (`tools/ab-idle.py`): CPU time of the named
  processes and of the system over 60 s, the GPU's busy share, the
  processes' PSS.
- item's own `sfduo-perfcheck` on phosh, for its dock and grid numbers.

phosh ran as it is used day to day, unlocked, nothing open; item-compositor
through `tools/session-run.sh`, nothing open. The phosh runs were taken
with the phone untouched (`tools/ab-phosh.sh`).

## What came out

**A tap** (the probe, 37 taps, at a spacing that is no multiple of the
frame, so every phase of it):

**Corrected in step 22.** This section took the first present after the
probe's commit as the frame holding it, and so found phoc 3 ms quicker
(4.3 against 7.6 ms). Under phoc each tap has two presents: the first,
drawn on the touch itself before the client's frame is in, and the one
with the content, 17.6 ms after the touch (median). item-compositor's
7.6 ms (6.2 from step 22) is the frame with the content: it is the quicker
by 10-11 ms (`log/2026-10-01-step-22-canvas.md`).

| | phosh (phoc) | item-compositor |
|---|---|---|
| touch to the client's commit | 3.1 ms | 0.4 ms |
| touch to the present with the content, median (p90) | 17.6 (24.4) ms | 7.6 (9.3) ms |

**The shell's own motion** (the finger pulls the left shade down and
back, opens the app grid and closes it, twice; the frames presented from
the gesture's start to 0.6 s after it ends):

| | phosh | item-compositor |
|---|---|---|
| shade down | 55-56 frames, gap median 16.7-16.8 ms, max 25-27, 1-2 gaps over 25 ms | 32-36 frames, 16.9-17.5, max 28-43, 1-5 (the first gesture after start) |
| shade back up | 41-42, 17.0-17.1, max 54, **6 gaps** over 25 ms each time | 38-39, 16.4-16.6, max 22-29, 0-1 |
| grid up | 44-45, 16.6, max 30-36, 1-2 | 49-50, 16.7, max 34-35, 3 |
| grid down | 26-28, 16.7, **max 235-243 ms**, 2-3 | 47, 16.6-16.7, max 34, 1-2 |

On phosh the shade stutters on its way back and the grid freezes for a
quarter of a second going down, each time. Ours keeps the vsync through
both, with a missed frame or two at the start of a slide.

**At rest, screen on, 60 s:**

| | phosh | item-compositor |
|---|---|---|
| the shell's processes | phoc, phosh, sfduo-dock, -system, -pen | item-compositor |
| their CPU | 3.5 % of a core (phoc 1.4, dock 1.8) | 2.6 % |
| their memory (PSS) | 461 MB (phoc 215, phosh 117, dock 73, system 31, pen 26) | 173 MB |
| the keyboard (stevia) | 40 MB | 56 MB |
| GPU busy | 0.2 % | 0.0 % |

item's services beside the shell cost more: sfduo-posture 8.8 % of a
core at rest, sfduo-brightness 0.2 %, sfduo-fingerprint 0; they have no
counterpart here yet (below).

**A client animating every frame** (the probe's `anim`), item-compositor:
57-61 frames a second, 0-4 vsyncs missed a second, commit to screen two
frames (32.5 ms: its frame callback comes as a frame is drawn, and its
next frame waits for the next). Under phoc the probe got no times (below).

**sfduo-perfcheck** on phosh, for the record: all 7 within thresholds;
dock crossing 16.2-16.6 ms a frame, grid 16.2-16.6 (22.7 on the first open,
max 49), Calculator's launch 0.8 s signal to toplevel.

**A launch**, item-compositor: GNOME Calculator 1.40-1.65 s from exec to
its window mapped (a warm session, three runs). Not yet comparable with
perfcheck's 0.8 s, which counts to the toplevel's creation, before its
first frame. GTK logs a portal refusal on start here ("Unable to find app
id": the app is not in a scope of its own), to be looked into.

## wp_presentation

`wp_presentation` is new here: the windows' and layer surfaces' feedback
is taken with each swapped frame and answered at the vsync it shows at,
in CLOCK_MONOTONIC, hwcomposer's own. GTK times its frames with it.

phoc on hwcomposer answers every feedback "discarded": its backend does
not report presentation, so under phosh GTK gets no frame times. This is
why the tap was timed at hwcomposer's present and not by the probe alone.

## What follows

1. A cheaper frame: draw only the damage, into a buffer of our own, and
   copy the whole of it out (step 7's item 3). Done in step 22.
2. Rest: no vsync from hwcomposer while nothing moves (60 wake-ups a
   second now).
3. A launch through a proper app scope (systemd-run --user --scope with
   the app's id), then launch timed against phosh the same way.
4. The gaps against item on phosh: posture (the Duo folded or flat, one
   panel), fingerprint unlock, automatic brightness, the polkit agent and
   the prompts for Wi-Fi and keyring passwords that phosh provides, calls
   over the lock screen, suspend and wake, installing as a real session
   with a way back to phosh.
