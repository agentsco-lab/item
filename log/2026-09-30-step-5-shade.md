# Step 5: the shade, drawn by the compositor

The first piece of the shell: a sheet over each panel, pulled down from the
panel's top edge by the finger (`compositor/src/shade.rs`).

## What it is

- A touch that starts in the top 20 logical px of a panel, or on its open
  sheet, belongs to the sheet and never reaches a client. The sheet's bottom
  edge follows the finger.
- Let go: faster than 0.3 px/ms and the sheet runs the way the finger went,
  else open if pulled past half the panel. The run takes 250 ms, easing out,
  and is drawn where it will be at the vsync the frame aims for. A tap on an
  open sheet closes it.
- The sheet is solid rectangles in the same GPU pass as the windows
  (`SolidColorRenderElement` ahead of the space's elements): a translucent
  sheet, a handle, and a clock in seven segments. There is no text rendering
  yet. Each rectangle keeps its own id, so the damage tracker redraws only
  what moved.
- The report adds `shade->screen`: from the kernel's time of a finger's move
  (libinput's, CLOCK_MONOTONIC) to the vsync the frame showing it aims for.
  `from the loop` counts from when the move reached the compositor.

## Results (a finger by hand, both panels)

| | |
|---|---|
| finger's move to the screen | 22-26 ms mean, 30 ms at most |
| the same from the compositor's loop | 20-23 ms |
| frames missed while pulling and running | 0 |
| a frame | about 2.5 ms to draw, about 3 ms to present |

For comparison, a tap to GTK Calculator's answer on screen is about 39 ms
(step 4b). The shade answers the finger in the next frame after it reaches
the loop, plus the frame's own time: move → loop about 2 ms, then half a
frame of waiting on average, the late draw, and the vsync.

Quick and smooth by hand.

The 12-16 missed frames in the last second of each run come from the
session closing (presents of 11-14 ms), not from the shade.

## A green line over the sheet

The first run showed a flickering green line, one pixel thick, over both
sheets while they were out. The sheet was placed in logical px, starting at
`-(h / 2)` from its physical bottom edge `h`. With `h` odd, that is one
physical pixel below the panel's top. The line was never drawn over. It kept
whatever each of the three buffers last held there, so it flickered, and
nothing under the sheet explains the green. The sheet is now a physical
rectangle from the panel's top to `h`. Second run: no line.
